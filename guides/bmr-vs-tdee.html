BMR vs TDEE: What's the Difference?

BMR and TDEE are two numbers that often get mixed up. Both estimate energy use, but they answer different questions. This guide explains each one and which to use when planning your calories.

Quick summary
  • BMR is the energy your body needs at complete rest.
  • TDEE is your total daily energy use, including movement, exercise and digestion.
  • For calorie planning, TDEE is the number to start from, not BMR.

What is BMR?

Basal metabolic rate is the energy your body uses to keep you alive at rest: breathing, circulation, temperature control and cell repair. It is usually the largest part of daily energy use. Estimate yours with the BMR calculator.

What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure is everything your body uses in a day. It includes BMR, the energy used to digest food, everyday movement such as walking and chores, and planned exercise. Use the TDEE calculator to estimate it from your BMR and activity level.

How they connect

TDEE = BMR × activity factor. The factors range from 1.2 for a sedentary lifestyle to 1.9 for very hard training or a physical job. For example, a BMR of 1,600 kcal at a factor of 1.55 gives a TDEE of about 2,480 kcal. A full walkthrough is in how to calculate your TDEE.

Which should you use?

  • Use TDEE as your maintenance calories, then adjust slightly up or down for your goal.
  • Use BMR to understand your baseline. It is not a daily eating target, and eating far below it for long periods is generally not advised without professional guidance.

For goals, continue with the daily calorie needs guide, the calorie goal planner or the daily calorie calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMR the same as RMR?

They are very close. RMR (resting metabolic rate) is measured under less strict conditions, so it is usually slightly higher. Calculators generally treat them as similar.

Can I raise my BMR?

BMR is mostly set by body size, age and body composition. Building muscle can raise it a little, but the effect is modest. Increasing daily movement usually changes TDEE more.

How accurate are these formulas?

They are estimates. Individual metabolism varies, so use the result as a starting point and adjust based on real-world results over a few weeks.
