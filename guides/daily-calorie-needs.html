How Many Calories Do You Need Per Day?

Your daily calorie need is the amount of energy your body uses in a day. It depends on your age, sex, height, weight and how active you are. This guide explains how the estimate works, how to adjust it for a goal, and why the result is a starting point rather than an exact rule.

Quick summary
  • Maintenance calories are your BMR multiplied by an activity factor.
  • A moderate change of about 250–500 kcal per day is commonly used for gradual weight change.
  • Calculators give estimates. Adjust based on how your body responds over several weeks.

Step 1: Estimate your BMR

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the energy your body uses at rest. A widely used formula is Mifflin–St Jeor: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5 for males, or − 161 instead of + 5 for females. Our BMR calculator does this for you.

Step 2: Add your activity level

Multiply your BMR by an activity factor to estimate maintenance calories, also called TDEE.

Activity levelFactor
Sedentary (little or no exercise)1.2
Lightly active (1–3 days/week)1.375
Moderately active (3–5 days/week)1.55
Very active (6–7 days/week)1.725
Extra active (hard training or physical job)1.9

The TDEE calculator applies these factors. For a step-by-step walkthrough, see how to calculate your TDEE.

Worked example

Take a 30-year-old man, 70 kg and 170 cm. BMR = 700 + 1,062.5 − 150 + 5 ≈ 1,618 kcal. At a moderately active factor of 1.55, maintenance is about 2,508 kcal per day. For a gentle weight-loss phase, a 500 kcal deficit would be about 2,000 kcal per day.

Step 3: Adjust for your goal

  • Maintain weight: eat around your TDEE.
  • Lose weight: a modest deficit, often 250–500 kcal below maintenance.
  • Gain weight: a modest surplus, often 250–500 kcal above maintenance.

About 7,700 kcal is commonly used as the energy equivalent of 1 kg of body weight, but real results vary because the body adapts. The calorie goal planner gives a daily target and a rough timeline. Or get everything in one step with the daily calorie calculator.

Step 4: Turn calories into a plan

Once you have a calorie target, you can divide it into protein, carbohydrates and fat. Read the protein, carbs and fat guide or use the macro calculator.

Why estimates can be off

  • Metabolism varies between people of the same age, height and weight.
  • Activity levels are hard to rate accurately.
  • Food labels and portion sizes are approximate. See why food calories vary.

Track your weight and energy for 2–4 weeks, then adjust your intake by small steps if needed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum calorie intake?

There is no single safe number for everyone. Very low calorie diets should only be followed under medical supervision.

Do men and women need different calories?

On average yes, mainly because of differences in body size and composition. The formula includes sex for this reason.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

If you choose an activity level that already includes your exercise, you generally do not need to add more. Keep it simple and adjust from results.
