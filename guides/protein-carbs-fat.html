Protein, Carbs and Fat Explained: A Simple Macronutrient Guide

Protein, carbohydrates and fat are called macronutrients, or "macros". They are the three nutrients that provide calories and make up most of what you eat. This guide explains what each one does, how many calories it provides, and how to estimate a sensible daily amount.

Quick summary
  • Protein and carbohydrates provide about 4 calories per gram. Fat provides about 9 calories per gram.
  • Common reference ranges for adults are 10–35% of calories from protein, 45–65% from carbohydrates and 20–35% from fat.
  • There is no single perfect split. The best one fits your goals, health, activity and food preferences.

Calories per gram

Protein

Protein supports muscle, skin, enzymes and immune function. Good sources include eggs, fish, chicken, dairy, lentils, chickpeas, beans, tofu and nuts. Daily needs are often expressed per kilogram of body weight: about 0.8 g/kg is the general reference intake for healthy adults, while people who exercise regularly or train for strength often aim higher. You can estimate your own range with the protein calculator.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are your body's main source of quick energy. They come from grains, rice, bread, fruit, vegetables, potatoes and legumes. Choosing mostly high-fibre, minimally processed sources helps with digestion and steady energy. Your carb range depends on your calories and how active you are. Try the carb calculator to see a gram range for your day.

Fat

Fat is essential. It helps your body absorb certain vitamins and supports normal hormone function. Foods like olive oil, nuts, seeds, fish and avocado provide mostly unsaturated fats, which are generally recommended over large amounts of saturated fat. Because fat is energy dense, small portions add up quickly. Use the fat intake calculator to estimate a daily range.

How to turn percentages into grams

  1. Start with your daily calorie target, for example 2,000 kcal.
  2. Pick a split, for example 30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat.
  3. Multiply: 2,000 × 30% = 600 kcal protein, 800 kcal carbs, 600 kcal fat.
  4. Divide by calories per gram: protein 600 ÷ 4 = 150 g, carbs 800 ÷ 4 = 200 g, fat 600 ÷ 9 ≈ 67 g.

The macro calculator does this for you. If you don't know your calorie target yet, start with the daily calorie calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing perfect numbers. Macro targets are estimates. Being close most days is enough.
  • Ignoring food quality. Two diets with identical macros can differ a lot in fibre, vitamins and minerals.
  • Cutting a whole macro. Carbs and fat both have important roles. Large changes are best discussed with a healthcare professional or registered dietitian.
  • Forgetting total calories. Macros only work as a plan when your overall calories fit your goal. See the daily calorie needs guide.

Frequently asked questions

Which macro is most important?

All three matter. Protein is often prioritised first for fullness and muscle, but your total calories and overall food quality matter just as much.

Do I need to track macros?

No. It can be useful for specific goals or to learn portion sizes, but many people do well with simple habits. Stop tracking if it causes stress or an unhealthy relationship with food.

Are these numbers right for everyone?

No. They are general reference ranges for healthy adults. Pregnancy, medical conditions, age and medicines can change nutrition needs.
