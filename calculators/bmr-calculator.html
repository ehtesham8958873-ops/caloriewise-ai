How BMR is calculated

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation estimates resting energy expenditure as: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5 for males, and − 161 instead of + 5 for females. Read more in our guide: BMR vs TDEE explained.

What to do with your BMR

BMR is not the number of calories you should eat. It only covers resting needs. To include daily movement, multiply by an activity factor with the TDEE calculator, or use the daily calorie calculator for a goal-based estimate. You can then split your calories with the macro calculator.
