Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about CalorieWise AI nutrition tools.

1. Is CalorieWise AI free to use?

Yes, CalorieWise AI offers free online nutrition calculators, including daily calorie, BMR, TDEE, protein, carbohydrate, fat, macro and meal calorie calculators. You can use these tools without paying or creating an account.

2. How accurate is the calorie calculator?

Our calorie calculator provides an estimate using your age, sex, height, weight and activity level. It uses established equations, but your actual calorie needs may differ due to metabolism, health, lifestyle and other factors.

3. What is BMR?

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate. It estimates the number of calories your body needs to perform basic functions while resting, such as breathing, circulation and maintaining body temperature.

4. What is TDEE?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It estimates the total calories you burn each day, including your resting energy needs, daily movement, exercise and other activities.

5. Can I use these tools on my phone?

Yes, CalorieWise AI is mobile-friendly and works on smartphones, tablets and desktop computers. You can use the free nutrition calculators directly in your web browser without installing an app.

6. Do you upload my calculator data?

Your calculator inputs are processed locally in your browser and are not uploaded to our website server by these calculators. However, third-party advertising or hosting services may collect certain information as described in our Privacy Policy.

7. Is this website a substitute for medical advice?

No. CalorieWise AI provides general educational information and nutrition estimates only. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent medical conditions. Consult a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian for advice about your personal health needs.

About CalorieWise AI

Simple tools for learning about calories and nutrition.

CalorieWise AI offers free estimates for daily energy needs, food calories and macronutrients. The calculators use common equations and approximate food reference values. They are intended for general education and planning.

How are calorie needs estimated?

The daily calorie and BMR tools use the Mifflin–St Jeor equation as an estimate of resting energy expenditure. TDEE is estimated by multiplying BMR by an activity factor. Individual energy needs can differ.
